What the reconstruction lost

    Plate IDs were assigned by the source against the models it used. Where a plate's rotation chain does not reach a suture's own age, the suture is dropped or its window clamped rather than drawn at its present-day position — which is what asking for a rotation outside a plate's defined range silently returns. The two models lose different sutures, so part of any gap between their curves in deep time is model coverage, not geology.

    Sources

    Sutures, activity intervals, plate-ID assignments and the glacial record: Macdonald, Swanson-Hysell, Park, Lisiecki & Jagoutz (2019), Science 364, 181–184, as published in the authors' Arc_Continent_Analysis repository. Latitude bands ±10/15/20/40° are that dataset's own columns.

    Reconstructions: Merdith et al. (2021); Torsvik & Cocks (2017), CEED. Plate IDs are the source's own — its attribute table carries one column per frame (PLATEID1, PLATEID_CE) and this page assigns none. Which column belongs to which model was established by test, not by the column names. Selecting a model swaps its continents, boundaries and sutures together; Torsvik & Cocks has no topologies, so no plate boundaries are drawn for it. Continent polygons include the submerged shelf, so they run wider than a coastline.
